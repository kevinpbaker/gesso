A painted node's picture is made at the scale it lands at, the device pixel ratio times any zoom above it, instead of at the device pixel ratio alone. Text, lines and plots drawn with `paint` stay sharp under a zoom: in Calcwright, math and drawings at 300% on a 2x display had been a 2x bitmap stretched three times. While the scale moves (a pinch, a zoom animation, a morph), the picture it has is stretched as before; once the scale has held for 120 ms it is made again, a frame's worth at a time, and the renderer asks for that frame through `nextRepaint`. A picture that would be larger than the surface or 4 million pixels at its zoom is replayed from its recording onto each frame instead of kept. Bitmaps above the device scale together hold at most one surface's worth of pixels (at least 32 MB), and the ones drawn longest ago are let go first. At 100% nothing changes. `PaintBox.scale` now includes the zoom, so a `1 / scale` hairline stays one device pixel. `PaintPictureCache.draw` takes the frame's clock and returns whether it drew a stretched picture; it has `beginFrame`, `endFrame`, `nextRepaint` and `zoomedMemory`. WebGPU still makes pictures at the device scale.
